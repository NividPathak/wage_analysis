# Methods

This file documents the design of the causal extension. Results are in [RESULTS.md](../RESULTS.md),
generated by code from `results/*.json`. Every judgment call, with a one-line reason, is in
[DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).

## Question

Did state minimum wage increases from 2011 to 2022 raise low-end wages, and did they reduce
employment in food service jobs?

## Data

**Outcomes: BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS), state files, May 2005 to May
2022.** The raw workbooks in `data/` (committed by the original team) are read with the team's column
alias map (`causal/oews_io.py`). States are keyed on the FIPS `AREA` code because the 2019 file has
no postal-code column. The team's `cleaned_data/` CSVs are not used because they drop the
All Occupations rows. Outcomes, all in logs:

| variable | definition | role |
|---|---|---|
| `log_p10_all` | 10th percentile hourly wage, All Occupations (00-0000) | wage bite |
| `log_median_food` | median hourly wage, Food Preparation and Serving (35-0000) | low-wage group wage |
| `log_emp_food` | total employment, Food Preparation and Serving (35-0000) | employment |
| `log_median_cs` | median hourly wage, Computer and Mathematical (15-0000) | placebo |
| `log_emp_all` | total employment, All Occupations | control / predictor |

SOC major groups are used because they are stable across the 2010 and 2018 SOC revisions. No outcome
value is suppressed for any state-year in 2005-2022 (see `docs/DATA_AUDIT.md`).

**Treatment: Vaghul and Zipperer (2022), v1.4.0, `mw_state_annual.xlsx`.** The effective minimum
wage is `max(annual average state minimum, annual average federal minimum)`. The panel covers the 50
states plus DC, 2005-2022 (918 state-years).

## Data limitation: OEWS three-year pooling

OEWS estimates for a given May reference year pool six semiannual survey panels collected over
three years. Year-to-year changes are therefore smoothed, and BLS cautions against treating OEWS as a
clean annual time series. For this study that has two consequences:

1. Effects are biased toward zero, because only part of each year's estimate reflects post-policy
   survey panels.
2. Timing is blurred, so an effect can appear to build up over two or three years after a policy
   change even if the true effect is immediate.

The analysis handles this by (a) reading event-study effects over several post-treatment years
rather than one, and (b) running a robustness check with the treatment lagged 1 and 2 years.

## Treatment definition (event study)

Implemented in `causal/treatment.py`; the saved table is `results/treatment_assignment.csv`.

- **Never-treated states:** the effective minimum wage equals the federal minimum ($7.25) in every
  year from 2010 to 2022.
- **Treated states:** states with a first "large" increase in 2011 or later, where large means the
  effective minimum (annual average) rises by at least $0.75 and at least 8% over the prior year.
  The treatment year g is the first such year.
- **Dropped states:** states already above the federal minimum in 2010 with no discrete large jump
  afterwards. They have no clean pre-period and are excluded from the event study and synthetic
  control (they stay in the continuous two-way fixed effects regressions, which use the full
  minimum wage variation).

## Estimators

### 1. Continuous two-way fixed effects (`causal/did.py`)

`y_st = beta * log_mw_st + state FE + year FE + e_st`, estimated with `pyfixest.feols`, standard
errors clustered by state (CRV1). With both sides in logs, beta is an elasticity. Robustness
versions: Census division x year fixed effects (absorbs regional shocks), treatment lagged 1 and 2
years (OEWS pooling), and `log_emp_all` as a control in the food employment regression.

### 2. TWFE event study

Relative-time dummies for event time -5 to +5 with the endpoints binned (<= -5, >= +5) and -1 as
the reference year; never-treated states have every dummy equal to zero. State and year fixed
effects, state-clustered SEs. Under staggered adoption with heterogeneous effects, TWFE uses
already-treated states as controls for later-treated ones and can put negative weights on some
comparisons (Goodman-Bacon 2021), which is why it is shown next to Callaway and Sant'Anna.

### 3. Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021)

Group-time effects ATT(g, t) are 2x2 DiDs comparing cohort g with never-treated states, using the
change from year g - 1 (universal base period). They are aggregated to event time (cohort-size
weights) and to one overall ATT (the "simple" aggregation: average over all post-treatment (g, t)
cells, weighted by cohort size). The `differences` package (0.3.0) is the main estimator, with 999
bootstrap draws and seed 42. `causal/cs_did.py` is an independent implementation (state-resampling
bootstrap, 999 draws, seed 42) that matches the package's point estimates to about 1e-16, is unit
tested on simulated data with a known effect of 0.10, and supplies the bootstrap covariance for the
CS pre-trend test.

### 4. Pre-trend tests

Joint test that the leads at event times -5, -4, -3 and -2 are all zero: a cluster-robust F test for
the TWFE event study and a Wald chi-square test with the bootstrap covariance for CS.

### 5. Robustness: pre-COVID sample

CS re-estimated on years up to 2019 and cohorts treated by 2019. For the early cohorts, event times
+4 and +5 fall in 2019-2022, when pandemic restrictions affected food service differently across
states.

### 6. Synthetic control (`causal/synth.py`)

Case: the treated state with the largest percentage jump in its treatment year that still leaves at
least 5 pre-treatment years. Donor pool: the never-treated states. Predictors: means of
`log_p10_all` over three equal pre-period sub-windows and the pre-period mean of `log_emp_all`.
Fitted with `pysyncon` (Abadie, Diamond and Hainmueller 2010): donor weights are non-negative and sum
to one, and predictor importance weights V are chosen to minimize pre-period outcome error.
Inference is placebo-in-space: each donor is refit as if treated in the same year, the post/pre
RMSPE ratio is computed for every unit, and the permutation p-value is the treated state's rank
divided by the number of units.

### 7. Power analysis (`causal/power.py`)

500 placebo draws (seed 42) using only never-treated states. Each draw gives fake treatment to the
same share of states as the real design, with treatment years drawn from the real cohort-year
distribution. A known effect delta is added to post-treatment outcomes, and the binary static DiD
(`y ~ post | state + year`, CRV1) is estimated. Power is the share of draws with p < 0.05; the
minimum detectable effect (MDE) is the smallest delta with 80% power; the delta = 0 rejection rate
is the false positive rate. Because adding delta * post shifts the OLS estimate by exactly delta and
leaves the clustered SE unchanged, each draw is fit once and evaluated for every delta.

### 8. Falsification

Computer and math median wages should not respond to the minimum wage. Every estimator above is also
run on `log_median_cs`, and all estimates are reported whichever way they come out.

```
make all     # data -> panel -> analysis -> report -> tests
```
